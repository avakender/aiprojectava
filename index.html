AI can write the answer. Someone still has to check it.

Artificial intelligence is already in search engines, phones, banks and classrooms. This page explains what it is, what it does well, where it fails, and why human checking matters more as it spreads.

What AI is

AI is software that performs tasks we usually link to human thinking, such as recognising images, translating languages or writing text. Most modern systems are not given rules to follow. They learn patterns from huge amounts of example data, a method called machine learning.

  1. DataText, images and records gathered from many sources.
  2. TrainingThe system adjusts itself until it predicts the patterns well.
  3. ModelA large set of numbers that stores what it learned.
  4. OutputA prediction: the next word, a label, an image.
  5. Human checkA person tests whether the output is true, fair and safe.

Fact Chatbots such as ChatGPT and Claude are generative AI. They predict a likely continuation of your text, which makes them fluent but not automatically correct.

Where it helps

  • Study and work. Summarising a reading, explaining a concept a second way, drafting an email or finding a bug in code.
  • Accessibility. Automatic captions, translation and text-to-speech open content to more people.
  • Health and science. Tools can flag patterns in scans or sort large research datasets, while specialists make the final decision.

Interpretation The best uses save time on a first draft while a person keeps responsibility for the result.

Where it fails

Errors
Generative models can state false things with confidence. These mistakes are often called hallucinations.
Bias
Systems copy patterns in their training data, including unfair ones.
Privacy
Text typed into a tool may be stored or reused under the provider’s terms.
Fraud
Synthetic voice and video make impersonation cheaper and more convincing.

Case study: the deepfake video meeting

Fact In early 2024, Hong Kong police said a finance worker joined a video call with what looked like the company’s chief financial officer and colleagues. All the others were deepfakes. He made 15 transfers worth about US$25.6 million. The firm, Arup, later confirmed that fake voices and images were used.

Interpretation Seeing a face and hearing a voice is no longer proof of identity. Confirm large payments through a second, known channel.

What could come next

Case study: AI and the power grid

Fact The International Energy Agency projects that data-centre electricity demand will more than double by 2030, to about 945 terawatt-hours. That is slightly more than Japan uses today.

Fact PJM, the largest US grid operator, expects data centres to account for nearly all of the 32 gigawatts of demand growth it forecasts through 2030. It has warned of possible power shortages as early as 2027 unless supply grows.

Prediction If new power plants and cables arrive more slowly than demand, bills will rise and local shortages become more likely. Efficiency gains could soften this.

Dependence

Prediction When many services rely on a few cloud providers, one outage can stop many things at once. Organisations will need manual fallbacks.

Supervision

Prediction AI agents that run code or send messages by themselves make errors travel faster. Approval steps, logs and limits will matter as much as model quality.

Interpretation The useful question is less “is AI good or bad?” than “who is checking, and could they still do the task without it?”

How this website was made

I built the site by hand as three files: index.html, styles.css and a small script.js. I chose a single narrow column because the topic needs reading, not browsing. The coloured Fact, Interpretation and Prediction labels keep evidence apart from opinion, as the assignment asks. The “how AI is built” diagram is an ordered list styled with CSS, so screen readers get real list structure instead of a picture.

I used semantic landmarks, a skip link, visible keyboard focus and tested contrast. After testing on a phone I changed the diagram to stack vertically. script.js only marks the current section in the menu, and the page works without it.
